# Reponse to Matthew Sequeira's Evaluating Design Blog

I also found the Mr. Beast example really interesting! I would not have thought that such a minor change could lead to great differences in views. Here are my thoughts on your blog:

## Feedback

- You did a great job of explaining the effects of Booking.com's inclusion of urgent messages, and the psychological reason behind them.

- I liked how this example was different from the example in lecture in that it was focused more on profit than aesthetics. It is important to keep in mind the different domains that A/B testing addresses.

- I also like how this is an example of a well-established company that uses frequent A/B testing. This was enlightening because I had the preconception that A/B testing was to be used primarily before deploying a product, but what better way to identify opportunities than leveraging an already-existing customer base that is using the app in a regular environment as opposed to a controlled environment!

## New Ideas and Insights

- I personally do not like it when sites try to get users to make impulsive decisions, especially when it comes to making purchases. That said, there are indeed valid instances in which conveying a sense of urgency is necessary, like when the user _needs_ to purchase a flight as soon as possible. A good question to ponder is whether or not this is a good measure of success. It is a financial success for the company, but how about in providing quality service to the user?

- Along those lines, I wonder if there are better ways to help users finalize bookings. For example, what normally prevents me from quickly purchasing a plane ticket is inadequate information, unclear cancellation/refund policies, price concerns, and trust issues with how the services are offered through the website. This is why I like American Airlines because I notice that it clearly and concisely lays out different plane ticket price options and relevant policies as I am selecting my flight or before I make the final purchase. The neat thing is that it does not feel like I am doing additional work, and at the end I feel like I have made a well-informed decision. I attribute my being a return American Airlines customer to this type of user interface.
